Frequently Asked Questions (Water Heater Not Heating)

How many showers can the Water Heater Not Heating run at once?

Under mild summer inlet water conditions requiring a 50°F rise, it outputs 7.6 GPM, comfortably supplying three simultaneous 2.5 GPM low-flow showers or four 1.8 GPM water-saver heads; in extreme winter conditions requiring a 75°F rise, output drops to 5.1 GPM, which supports two 2.5 GPM showers simultaneously.

How does groundwater temperature affect the Water Heater Not Heating?

Continuous flow capacity is inversely proportional to thermal rise, calculated as GPM = (BTU/hr × Efficiency) / (ΔT × 500.4). With incoming winter groundwater at 45°F heating to 120°F (75°F ΔT), flow throttles to 5.1 GPM, whereas 70°F incoming groundwater heating to 120°F (50°F ΔT) allows an unrestricted 7.6 GPM flow rate.

What gas line or breaker size is needed for the Water Heater Not Heating?

This unit requires a minimum 3/4-inch black iron or corrugated stainless steel tubing (CSST) gas supply line sized for 199,000 BTU/hr, a standard 120V/15A dedicated electrical circuit for the internal controls and freeze protection (drawing under 3 amps), and 2-inch or 3-inch PVC, CPVC, or polypropylene direct venting.
